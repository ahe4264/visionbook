Electron interference: the double-slit experiment

Electrons fired at a barrier with two narrow slits build up an interference pattern on a detection screen. (a) shows the wave description — expanding wavefronts from the two slits overlap and interfere. (b) shows the same experiment as individual particle detections accumulating in time. Both panels use exactly the same geometry, so the bright fringes in (a) sit at the same heights as the dot bands in (b).

What to look for. Each electron arrives at a single point — a particle. Yet the places it can land are set by a wave: the probability of landing at height y is |ψ(y)|², the same two-slit interference intensity drawn in panel (a). Early in the exposure the hits look random; after thousands of electrons the fringes appear. Drag slit separation and watch the wave fringes and the dot bands change spacing together — widening d squeezes the fringes (Δy = λL/d), and a longer wavelength (slower electron, since λ = h/mv) spreads them out. This is the effect Davisson and Germer measured for electrons scattering from a nickel crystal, confirming de Broglie's matter waves.
