Viscosity of motor oils — the falling-ball comparison

Four identical steel balls are released at the same instant into four cylinders of motor oil. A more viscous oil exerts more drag, so the ball settles to a lower terminal speed and has fallen a shorter distance at any given moment. Positions here are integrated from the real equation of motion, not drawn by hand.

Model. Steel ball (d = 16.0 mm, ρ = 7800 kg/m³) released from rest in a 40.0 mm bore cylinder holding a 300 mm column of oil (ρ = 890 kg/m³). Motion is integrated with RK4 from meff dv/dt = (m − mf)g − 3πηdv·W·(1 + 0.15 Re0.687): weight minus buoyancy minus Stokes drag, with the Schiller–Naumann correction for finite Reynolds number, the Ladenburg–Faxén wall factor W = 2.166 for d/D = 0.4, and added fluid mass ½mf. Viscosities follow η = 2.0·e0.0316(SAE−20) Pa·s, which reproduces the viscosity ratios of the SAE J300 grades (cold oil, ≈0 °C). Every ball position, speed and label on this page comes from that integration; Re stays below 1.4, so the flow is in the Stokes-dominated regime the textbook picture assumes.
