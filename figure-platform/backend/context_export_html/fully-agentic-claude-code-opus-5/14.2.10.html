The solid is bounded by the three coordinate planes and the slanted plane. Its shadow on the xy-plane is the triangle R, the region of the double integral. Drag the red strip in the right panel to see the inner integral appear as a vertical cross-section of the solid.

Inner integral (area of the red cross-section at x = 0.550):  ∫0 2−2x0 (4 − 4x0 − 2y) dy = (2 − 2x0)2 = 0.810
Cross-section at height z = 0.00:  A(h) = ½ · 4−h⁄4 · 4−h⁄2 = (4 − h)2⁄16 = 1.000
Volume  V = ∫01 ∫0 2−2x (4 − 4x − 2y) dy dx = ∫01(2−2x)2dx = 4⁄3 ≈ 1.3333  (= ∫04A(h) dh, the same volume sliced the other way)

The red triangle in the 3D picture is the slice of the solid by the plane x = x0; its area is the value of the inner integral. Sweeping x0 from 0 to 1 sweeps out the whole solid — that is the outer integral.
