Manometers: reading gas pressure from a mercury column

A manometer compares the pressure of a trapped gas with a known pressure at the other end of a U‑tube. The mercury simply settles until the two arms balance, and the height difference h between the two mercury surfaces is what you read off. Pick a configuration, then drag the pressure slider and watch the mercury — and the value of h — respond.

Tip: click the h arrow inside any panel for a step‑by‑step derivation of that panel's equation.

    Mercury density ρ = 13 534 kg/m3 (25 °C), g = 9.80665 m/s2, Patm fixed at 760 torr = 101.3 kPa. Column heights are computed from h = ΔP / ρg and drawn to scale (0.25 px per mm of mercury); mercury is conserved, so each surface moves by h/2. The torr is defined with 0 °C mercury (13 595 kg/m3), which is slightly denser than the 25 °C mercury used here — that is why 760 torr holds up 763 mm rather than exactly 760 mm.
