The plenoptic function L(x, y, θ)

The plenoptic function records the intensity of the light ray arriving at every point of a scene from every direction. This 2‑D slice samples it at four places: two in free space, where light arrives from essentially every direction, and two on the film of a pinhole camera, where the opaque body blocks everything except the single narrow bundle of rays that squeezes through the aperture. Click or drag anywhere in the scene to move the sample point; every ray shown is traced against the real scene geometry.

Angular distribution L(θ)

Why this matters. Knowing L everywhere means knowing every photograph that could ever be taken of the scene: a camera is just a device that samples a thin slice of the plenoptic function. Enlarge the pinhole above and each film point starts integrating a cone of directions instead of one ray — brighter, but blurred. A neural radiance field (NeRF) is a network trained to output exactly this function, L(x, y, z, θ, φ), so that new views can be rendered by querying it along camera rays.
