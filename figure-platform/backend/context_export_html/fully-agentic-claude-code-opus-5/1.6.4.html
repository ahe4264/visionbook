Why tan x ≥ x ≥ sin x  —  the area sandwich behind limx→0 (sin x)/x = 1

In the unit circle the angle x (in radians) produces three nested regions on the same base of length 1: the big right triangle of height tan x, the circular sector of angle x, and the small triangle of height sin x. Their areas are computed directly from x, so the inequality below is genuine, not drawn by hand.

Dividing tan x/2 ≥ x/2 ≥ sin x/2 through by sin x/2 (positive for 0 < x < π/2) gives 1/cos x ≥ x/sin x ≥ 1, and taking reciprocals reverses it into the form above. Since cos x → 1 and 1/cos x → 1 as x → 0, the Squeezing Theorem forces (sin x)/x → 1. The same picture reflects to x < 0 because (sin x)/x is even.
