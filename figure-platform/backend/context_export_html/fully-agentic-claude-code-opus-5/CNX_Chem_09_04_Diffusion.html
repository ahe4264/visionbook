Diffusion of gases: H2 and O2 mixing through a stopcock

Two bulbs, one filled with H2 and one with O2, are joined by a stopcock. When the stopcock is opened, random molecular motion carries each gas down its own concentration gradient until both gases are spread uniformly. Because H2 is 16× lighter than O2, its molecules move about 4× faster and mix first — the observation behind Graham's law.

Bold = average of 16 independent runs; faint = the single run animated on the left.

Model: 30 H2 and 30 O2 molecules, hard-wall two-bulb geometry with a narrow neck. Each molecule travels in a straight line and changes direction after roughly one mean free path (30 model units), giving a diffusion coefficient D = uλ/2 that is 4× larger for H2 (the speed ratio 3.98 is rounded to 4 sub-steps per frame). "Crossed" counts the molecules now in the bulb they did not start in; at a uniform mixture that fraction is 50 %.
