Axial vs. equatorial positions in a trigonal bipyramid — why ClF3 is T-shaped

Five electron pairs around Cl adopt a trigonal bipyramidal electron-pair geometry. The two lone pairs can sit in three different ways. Only one of them is observed — and the geometry itself tells you which.

Every angle and count in this table is computed from the 3-D unit vectors of the five trigonal-bipyramidal positions (two along ±z, three at 120° in the xy-plane), not typed in. Repulsion ranks lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair, and only 90° contacts matter (120° and 180° contacts are far apart).

Figure. (a) In a trigonal bipyramid the two axial positions lie directly opposite one another on the vertical axis, while the three equatorial positions form a triangle around the middle. (b–d) The two lone pairs of ClF3 (red lines) have several possible arrangements; the left drawing of each pair shows where the lone pairs sit, the right drawing shows the resulting molecular structure. The T-shaped structure (b) is the one actually observed, consistent with both lone pairs occupying the roomier equatorial positions.
