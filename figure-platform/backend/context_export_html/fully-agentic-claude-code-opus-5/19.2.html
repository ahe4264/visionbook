Figure 19.2  A Fibonacci heap H, shown two ways

(a) the abstract view — five min-heap-ordered trees whose roots form a circular root list (drawn as a dashed line), with H.min pointing at the minimum root; (b) the same heap as it is actually stored — every sibling list is a circular, doubly linked list, and every node carries p, child, left, right, degree and mark. Black nodes are marked: they have lost one child since they last became the child of another node.

(a) Abstract view. Each tree is min-heap ordered; the dashed line is the circular root list. The trees are drawn side by side but their roots are all siblings in one list.
(b) The same heap, drawn with its real pointers. Arrows above a row are right pointers, arrows below are left pointers, and each list closes back on itself. Vertical/diagonal arrows pointing up are p pointers; the single arrow pointing down out of a node is its child pointer — a parent points at just one of its children, and the rest are reached through that child's sibling list.
