Critical mass: why the size of a fissile sample decides whether a chain reaction survives

Each fission releases neutrons. A neutron either travels far enough to leave the sample (it is lost) or it strikes another nucleus first and causes a new fission (it is productive). Because losses happen at the surface and fissions happen throughout the volume, a bigger sample loses a smaller fraction — the whole figure below is produced by an actual Monte Carlo neutron-transport calculation, not by drawing.
