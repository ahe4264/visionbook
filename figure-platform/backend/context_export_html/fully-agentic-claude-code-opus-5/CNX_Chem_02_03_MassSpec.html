Mass spectrometry of zirconium — how ion deflection becomes a spectrum

Sample vapour is ionized, accelerated through a potential V, and bent by a magnetic field B. The radius of each ion’s circular path is r = √(2mV/q) / B, so lighter ions are deflected most. Only ions that land on the collector slit reach the detector, so sweeping B scans the mass spectrum.

How the geometry is computed. Every ion path is a genuine circular arc: the ion enters the field region at one point moving horizontally, follows a circle of radius r = r₀√(m/m₀)/B, and the exit angle is found by solving the arc against the (tilted) far boundary of the pole gap. The straight drift afterwards is intersected with the detector plane, giving the landing offset u from the collector slit. Transmission is computed from u, the beam width and the slit width — nothing is faked or hard‑coded.

Because the Zr isotopes differ in mass by only 1–6 parts in 90, their real separation at the detector is a few percent of the orbit radius. The ×6 setting magnifies the mass differences (not the physics) so the five beams read clearly, matching the schematic style of the textbook figure; switch to ×1 to see the true, tiny separation and why real instruments need a narrow slit and precise field control.
