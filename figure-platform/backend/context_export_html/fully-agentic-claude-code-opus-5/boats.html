How far away is the boat?

Two classical ways of turning measured angles into a distance you cannot pace out. Method 1 uses one observer plus a known height; method 2 uses two observers separated by a known baseline. Method 2 is exactly what your two eyes do — it is binocular parallax, the root of stereo vision.

Why this matters. Both methods recover depth from a triangle whose base you already know — a height h in method 1, a separation t in method 2. The wider that base looks from the target, the larger the angles, and the sharper the estimate: watch the ± uncertainty figures collapse as you raise the observer or move the two observers apart. Your eyes run method 2 with a baseline of only t ≈ 6.5 cm, which is why stereo depth perception fades out after a few tens of metres and other depth cues have to take over.
