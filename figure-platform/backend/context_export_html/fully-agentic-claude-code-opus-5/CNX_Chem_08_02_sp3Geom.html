sp³ Hybridization: from four atomic orbitals to a tetrahedron

One s orbital and three p orbitals on the same atom are mixed mathematically to give four equivalent sp³ hybrid orbitals, each with one large lobe and one small back lobe, pointing at 109.5° to one another — the tetrahedral geometry behind CH4, NH3 and H2O. How the surfaces are drawn. Each lobe is the polar plot r(θ,φ) ∝ |ψ(θ,φ)|² of the angular part of the wavefunction — the angular probability density — built from the normalised real orbitals s → 1 and pk → √3 (k̂·r̂). (Plotting |ψ| itself would give the mathematically correct but unfamiliar pair of tangent spheres; squaring gives the usual elongated lobes without moving a single node.) For the full sp³ mix, ψ ∝ 1 + 3cos θ measured from the hybrid axis: it is maximal (+4) along the axis, passes through zero exactly at θ = 109.47°, and reaches −2 behind the atom — so every hybrid is drawn with a large front lobe and a back lobe (2/4)² = ¼ as long, of opposite sign. One single scale factor is used for every picture on the page, so the lobes really are comparable: the s sphere has radius K, a p lobe reaches 3K, and an sp³ hybrid reaches 4K. Colour encodes the sign of ψ, not charge.
