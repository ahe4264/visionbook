Perspective projection from similar triangles

A world point P = (X, Y, Z) and the pinhole at the origin O define a single ray. That ray meets the image plane Z = −f at the image point. Slicing the ray with the X–Z plane and the Y–Z plane gives two pairs of similar triangles, and those pairs are the projection equations x/f = X/Z and y/f = Y/Z.

Move the geometry

Drag the red point P to change X and Y; drag the small grey foot point on the green triangle to change X and depth Z.

The two similar-triangle relations

x / f  =  X / Z
y / f  =  Y / Z

Hence  x = f X/Z  and  y = f Y/Z. Both triangles share the same ray and the same pair of depths f and Z — that is why one ray fixes both image coordinates.

Readout

green = X–Z cross-section  ·  cyan = Y–Z cross-section

The real image forms behind the pinhole, on the plane Z = −f, and is therefore inverted: the image point is (−fX/Z, −fY/Z, −f) = (−f/Z) P. The labels x and y are the lengths of the marked red sides, i.e. the size of the image.
