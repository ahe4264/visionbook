Why a bare wall shows no picture — and a pinhole does

Light leaves every point of the tree in every direction. On a bare wall (a) each point of the wall collects rays from the whole scene at once, so the wall is uniformly washed — no image. Put an opaque barrier with a single small hole in between (b) and each wall point can only be reached from one scene direction: the one–to–one correspondence that makes an (inverted) image.
