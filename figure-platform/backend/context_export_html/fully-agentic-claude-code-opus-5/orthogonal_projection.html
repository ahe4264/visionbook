Orthographic (parallel) projection of a solid onto a projection plane

Projection rays run parallel to the Z axis and meet the plane at right angles. The footprint that appears on the plane therefore keeps exactly the lateral size of the object, no matter how far down the Z axis the object sits: x = X, y = Y. Slide towards perspective to watch that guarantee break.

What the geometry actually does. Every vertex of the staircase solid is a genuine 3-D point (X, Y, Z). The orthographic image of that vertex is (x, y) = (X, Y): the Z coordinate is simply discarded, so the projector is the line through the vertex parallel to Z, and the mapping is a true-size copy of the object's X–Y footprint. The perspective image of the same vertex is (x, y) = (X, Y) · e / (Z − z_eye), obtained by intersecting the plane Z = z_p with the line joining the vertex to the eye. Because that factor contains Z, near parts of the solid are magnified more than far parts: parallel edges stop being parallel in the image and lengths stop being metric. Only the red faces (perpendicular to Z) and the near cyan end cap turn towards the plane, so those are the surfaces that carry area into the image; the green treads are parallel to the projection direction and collapse to lines.
