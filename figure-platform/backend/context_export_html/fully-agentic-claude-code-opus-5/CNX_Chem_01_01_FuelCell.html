Hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell: how it turns a chemical reaction into a current

Hydrogen is oxidised at the anode, the proton-exchange membrane passes only H⁺, so the electrons are forced through the external circuit — that detour is the electric power. At the cathode the protons, the electrons and incoming oxygen recombine into water, the cell's only product.

Animate the cycle

The cell is running: every H₂ that reacts releases exactly 2 H⁺ and 2 e⁻, and every 2 H⁺ + 2 e⁻ + ½ O₂ leaves as one H₂O.

Half-reactions

Anode: 2 H₂ → 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻

Cathode: O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ → 2 H₂O

Overall: 2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O

E°cell ≈ 1.23 V. The membrane keeps the two half-reactions apart, which is what makes the chemical energy available as electrical work instead of heat.
