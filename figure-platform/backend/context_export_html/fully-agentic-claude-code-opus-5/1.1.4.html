The tangent line as the limiting position of secant lines

A secant line joins the fixed point P to a second point Q on the curve. As Q slides along the curve toward P, the secant rotates about P; the line it approaches is the tangent line at P. Panel (b) checks this general definition against the classical tangent to a circle — the line through P perpendicular to the radius.

Drag the blue points Q (moveable) and P (point of tangency) directly on either figure.

Why the limit is the right definition. The secant slope is the difference quotient msec = (f(xQ) − f(xP)) / (xQ − xP). It is undefined when Q = P (0/0), so the tangent cannot be built from one point alone — it is the limit the secants approach. In panel (b) the limiting line is computed exactly the same way, and it lands on the line through P perpendicular to the radius CP: the two definitions agree.
