The nonlinear latent variable model behind the VAE

A latent z ~ Norm[0,1] is pushed through a nonlinear decoder f[z,φ] to give the mean of a spherical Gaussian over the data x = (x1, x2). Every slice of the joint Pr(x, z|φ) is a simple Gaussian, but adding them all up — marginalizing over z — gives a curved, non‑Gaussian marginal Pr(x|φ) with no closed form. That intractable integral is why VAEs need variational inference.

What is computed. The decoder is a genuine tanh network f(z) = V·tanh(Wz + b) + c mapping the scalar latent to a point in the plane; the traced teal curve is its image. Each brown slice plane shows the joint density Pr(x, z) = Normx[f(z), σ²I] · Normz[0,1] on the same colour scale — which is why slices far from z = 0 are dimmer: the prior down-weights them. The right panel is the numerical quadrature Pr(x|φ) = ∫ Pr(x|z,φ) Pr(z) dz over 161 latent samples, and the posterior plot is Pr(z|x,φ) ∝ Pr(x|z,φ) Pr(z) evaluated along the dashed line of constant x drawn through the cube. Occlusion in the cube is solved exactly, so the curve is dashed precisely where a slice plane hides it.
