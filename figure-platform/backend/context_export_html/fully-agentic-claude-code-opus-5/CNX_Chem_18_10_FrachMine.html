The Frasch process — mining sulfur without going underground

Three concentric pipes are sunk into a buried sulfur bed. Superheated water melts the sulfur, compressed air lightens the molten column so it can be lifted, and a froth of sulfur + water + air rises to the surface. Drive the animation and change the operating conditions to see why each of the three pipes is necessary.

Operating conditions

Sulfur melts at 115.2 °C. Below that the deposit stays solid.
Must exceed the vapour pressure of water at that temperature, or the water flashes to steam.
Air bubbles cut the average density of the rising column.

Why it works

Sulfur melts at only 115.2 °C and liquid sulfur is immiscible with water, so the deposit can be melted in place and pumped out — no shafts, no ore, no chemistry. Because liquid sulfur (ρ ≈ 1790 kg/m³) is far denser than the water column driving it, a pure melt cannot be pushed all the way to the surface; the compressed air is what makes the lift possible. Everything that comes up is a physical mixture, so once the water and air separate at the surface the sulfur solidifies at 99.5–99.9 % purity with no refining at all.
