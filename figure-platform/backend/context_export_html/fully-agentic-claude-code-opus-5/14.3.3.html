The polar rectangle R : r1 ≤ r ≤ r2,  θ1 ≤ θ ≤ θ2

The building block for double integration in polar coordinates. Two circular arcs (constant r) and two rays (constant θ) bound a curved quadrilateral. Move the sliders to change the radial or angular bounds; switch on the straight-sided rectangle to see why R is only an analogue of a Cartesian rectangle.

Compare with a true rectangle

Its two straight sides of length Δr lie along the rays, but its other two sides are chords, not arcs — and the outer arc of R is longer than the inner one.
