Shape change = largest vertical displacement of the active-site contour away from its unbound form. Residual misfit = how far short of full depth the substrate would still sit if it were held directly over its pocket. Both are measured from the drawn contours: the substrate's lower surface is tested against the enzyme's current contour at every point, so a substrate physically cannot sink into a site that does not yet match it.

Both models explain specificity, but by different means. In the lock-and-key model (Fischer, 1894) the active site is a rigid pocket whose shape, charge and hydrogen-bonding groups already complement the substrate; a molecule of the wrong shape simply cannot enter. The shape-change readout stays at 0 for every value of the slider.

Induced fit (Koshland, 1958) keeps the same requirement for complementarity but places it at the end of binding: the free enzyme's site is open and only roughly matched, and contact with the correct substrate drives a conformational change that closes the protein around it. Notice that the substrate's residual misfit only falls to zero after the shape change happens — the fit is a consequence of binding, not a precondition for it. The final complex is the same in both panels.
